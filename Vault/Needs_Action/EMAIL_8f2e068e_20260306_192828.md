** Geopolitics & Geostrategy
------------------------------------------------------------

The dominant driver across all asset classes today is the escalating US-Israel military campaign against Iran, now in its sixth day. Strikes have targeted Iranian infrastructure, with Iran retaliating via attacks on tankers and potential drone disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz (handling ~20% of global oil flows). President Trump has signaled operations could last “weeks,” while reports emerged of a US submarine sinking an Iranian warship and Iran dismissing de-escalation talks.

Geostrategic ripple effects include:
* China is ordering major refiners to halt diesel/gasoline exports.
* Heightened concerns over energy supply chains, inflation pass-through, and potential regime-change dynamics in the region.
* Broader fragmentation: markets pricing in persistent risk premium on energy and defense, while some capital/talent flows are already shifting toward India as a perceived safer hub.

This has created a classic “risk-on/risk-off” whipsaw: oil and defense stocks surge, while travel, consumer, and growth names face pressure. Markets remain resilient (no freefall), but volatility (VIX elevated) is elevated as investors debate the duration of the conflict.


** Indices
------------------------------------------------------------

Global equities showed mixed but contained moves after yesterday’s partial rebound. Geopolitical jitters and rising oil weighed on sentiment, though strong prior services-sector data (quickest pace since mid-2022) and cooling price pressures provided some support.
* US (live ~10:30 AM ET): Dow Jones -1.27% at 48,118; S&P 500 -0.71% at 6,821; Nasdaq -0.53% at 22,687. (Prior close March 4: S&P +0.8% to ~6,869, Dow +0.5% to ~48,739, Nasdaq +1.3%).
* Europe: Broadly lower — STOXX 600 -0.49%, FTSE 100 -0.75%, DAX -0.70%, CAC 40 -0.74%.
* Asia (prior close): Resilient rebound — Nikkei +1.90%, Shanghai Composite +0.64%, Hang Seng +0.28%. South Korea’s Kospi surged ~11% in recovery mode after an earlier war-induced plunge.

Sector rotation continued: Energy, materials, utilities, and defense outperformed; airlines, tech (in spots), and consumer discretionary lagged.


** FX (Foreign Exchange)
------------------------------------------------------------

US Dollar strengthened as a safe-haven amid geopolitical uncertainty and rising inflation fears (DXY +0.46% to 99.23).
* EUR/USD -0.46% to 1.158
* GBP/USD -0.34% to 1.333
* USD/JPY +0.4% to 157.66
* AUD/USD -0.83% (commodity currencies under pressure)
* Indian Rupee strengthened to a 3-day high vs USD.


** Commodities
------------------------------------------------------------

Energy complex spiked on supply-disruption fears:
* Brent crude +3.0–4.9% (trading ~$83.85–$84 range; WTI similarly elevated near $78+).
* Tanker traffic and Qatar LNG flows disrupted; analysts warn of sustained premium if Hormuz risks persist.

Precious metals: Gold mixed intraday but broadly higher on safe-haven flows (spot ~$5,095–$5,166, up ~0.9% in some feeds; recent record territory above $5,100–$5,300). Silver softer. Copper and industrial metals are slightly down due to growth concerns.


** Overall Sentiment & Key Takeaways
------------------------------------------------------------

Markets are pricing in a contained but inflationary shock rather than systemic collapse. Yesterday’s strong US services PMI and falling price index helped cap downside, but today’s oil surge and ongoing Middle East headlines triggered fresh caution.
* Upside risks: Quick de-escalation or US escorts stabilizing energy flows.
* Downside risks: Prolonged Hormuz disruption → higher inflation → delayed Fed/ECB cuts.

Volatility remains the theme that traders are watching today’s US jobless claims, trade balance, and productivity data for domestic resilience signals. Energy, defense, and value/cyclical stocks are the clear relative winners; broad indices are holding but under pressure.
